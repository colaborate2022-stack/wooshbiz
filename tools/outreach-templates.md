# Link outreach templates

Not marketing copy — these are short, specific asks. The whole point is that
each one references something real (a link we already gave, an event we
actually ran), because a generic "please link to us" email gets deleted.

Send from a named person, not info@. Keep the subject short. Do not attach
anything.

---

## 1. Venues we already recommend

We list six venues on `/resorts.html` and now link to each of their sites.
That is the ask: we already did the thing we are requesting back.

Send to: the sales / banquet / MICE manager, not the general enquiry inbox.

> **Subject:** We've listed you on our venues page
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We're Woosh Biz — we run corporate team building and offsites, and we've
> been sending groups to [Venue] for a while now.
>
> We've just updated the venue page on our site and [Venue] is on it, with a
> link across to you: https://wooshbiz.com/resorts.html
>
> If you keep a list of activity or team building partners on your site,
> we'd appreciate a mention back. Happy to send across a short description
> and our logo if that's useful.
>
> Either way the listing stays up — we recommend you to clients regularly.
>
> [Your name]
> Woosh Biz | wooshbiz.com | +91 92108 04078

**Who to send to first:** Natventure and Visava — smaller operators, decision
made by one person, most likely to say yes. Club Mahindra and Westin are
large chains where this needs a regional contact and will usually go nowhere
without one; send anyway, expect nothing.

## 2. Corporate clients, after an event

Timing is everything here. Ask while the event is still fresh, ideally in the
thank-you email — not weeks later.

> **Subject:** Thanks for [date] — one small favour
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Really enjoyed running [activity] for the team on [date] — [one specific,
> genuine detail from the day].
>
> If you're posting about it on LinkedIn or the company blog, would you mind
> tagging us or linking to wooshbiz.com? Helps more than you'd think for a
> business our size.
>
> Photos from the day are here if you want them: [link]
>
> [Your name]

**Why this works:** you are not asking for a favour out of nowhere, you are
attached to something they already want to post about. Offering the photos is
what makes it easy for them to say yes.

## 3. Colleges, after a programme

College domains (.ac.in / .edu.in) carry real weight and almost every college
publishes an events or news page. Most will agree — nobody thinks to ask.

> **Subject:** Write-up for the [programme] session
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Glad the [leadership camp / cyber security session] went well with the
> [year / department] batch.
>
> If the session is going up on the college news or events page, could you
> include a line crediting Woosh Biz with a link to wooshbiz.com? I can send
> a short paragraph and photos so it's no extra work for you.
>
> [Your name]

**Ask the coordinator you actually worked with**, not the college's general
address — they are the one writing the post.

## 4. "Top team building companies in India" listicles

Search that phrase, note who ranks on page one, and check whether the article
takes submissions. Some are genuine editorial, some are paid placements.

Only pay where the article itself ranks — check it appears on page one for a
term an HR manager would actually search. A paid slot on a page nobody reads
is money gone.

> **Subject:** Woosh Biz — for your team building companies list
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Saw your piece on [title]. We're Woosh Biz, based in Mumbai — we run
> in-office and outdoor team building for corporates and colleges, and have
> worked with Accor, Westin, Mahindra and Aditya Birla among others.
>
> If you update the list, we'd be glad to be considered. Details here:
> https://wooshbiz.com
>
> [Your name]

---

## Rule of thumb

One real link from a venue or a client is worth more than fifty from paid
directories, and the paid ones can actively hurt. If an offer arrives
promising hundreds of backlinks, it is the bad kind.
